Yesterday, 50 members of Women Defining AI gathered for our monthly "Build TogetHER" session — and watched two of our own build a functioning Slack bot live, from scratch.

Avril Fuentes (product marketer) and Ruta Gadgil (senior front-end engineer) built BadgeBot: a Slack app that tracks learning progress and automatically generates a personalized PDF completion certificate the moment a member finishes a module.

Three months of work, distilled into one hour. Two key lessons they shared:

**1. Give your AI tool a visual, not a paragraph.**
Detailed wireframes and database schemas in the prompt outperformed plain English descriptions — every time.

**2. Break it into phases. One tab per phase.**
Asking an AI tool to build the whole thing at once is a recipe for lost context and broken builds. Small, testable phases keep everything coherent.

This is what "members teaching members" looks like in practice. No polished slides. No pretending it was easy. Just two builders sharing the real recipe — including the three messy months it took to get there.

That's the Build TogetHER spirit, and I couldn't be more proud of our community.
